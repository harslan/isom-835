ISOM 835 · AUC IN PLAIN WORDS
ISOM 835 · the number on every leaderboard

AUC is the score in a
head-to-head game

No formulas. One game, one picture, one sentence to remember.

The game · one round

Two guests. Who is riskier?

Guest A
Really canceled

The model does not know that.

Guest B
Really stayed

The model does not know that either.

Ask the model: "which of these two is riskier?"
If it points at the canceler, it wins the round.

The definition

Play every possible pair

AUC = the share of
rounds the model wins

Every canceler against every stayer. That is all it is.

Play it · 40 guests, lined up by the model's risk score
The model knows nothingThe model is perfect
Rounds won: 0 of 0
Press Play one round. The model wins if the canceler stands to the right of the stayer.
Three numbers to remember

How to read an AUC

A coin flip
0.50

Wins half the rounds. It knows nothing.

Last week's forest
0.942

Picks the right guest about 94 times out of 100.

Perfect
1.00

Never gets a pair wrong. (On real data: look for a leak.)

Why it is useful · 1

No cutoff needed

Accuracy
"First draw a line"

Above 50% counts as a cancel? Or 30%? Move the line and the grade changes.

AUC
Grades the line-up itself

Compare models first. Choose the cutoff later, from the costs (Session 5).

Why it is useful · 2

It can't be fooled by lopsided data

A hotel where only 5 in 100 guests cancel. A lazy model says: "nobody cancels."

Its accuracy
95%

Sounds great. Finds zero cancelers.

Its AUC
0.50

Same score for everyone: it cannot rank anybody.

Why we use it for the hotel

A manager's decisions are ranking decisions

  • "Which bookings are safest to count on?" The left end of the line-up.
  • "Which riskiest guests get a deposit request or a reminder call?" The right end.
  • A cleaner line-up serves every one of these. And it is the score Kaggle used for our competition.
The one thing AUC does not tell you

It checks the order, not the numbers

The model says
90%

for its riskiest guests.

In truth
60%

of them cancel. Same line-up, same AUC.

Are the percentages themselves right? That is calibration (Session 5).

The sentence to remember
AUC is how often the model
ranks a real canceler
above a real stayer.
